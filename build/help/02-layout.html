Finding your way around

The studio has the help on the left and five tabs on the right. The tabs share one set of files and one Python, so what you make in one tab is there in all the others.

The five tabs

Viewer
Any gpxil file as pixels, one for each base: the reference, a person’s genotype and variants, and the quality of the calls. Point at a pixel to see what the file holds there. The Viewer
Tools
A form for each job: make a gpxil file, check it, draw it, print it, get FASTA and VCF out of it, make pictures of genes and proteins. Each form also shows the same job as a command and as Python. Workflows, in the same tab, chain tools together. The Tools tab
Files
Your files and the examples, with a view for each kind: a gpxil file’s contents, a picture, a PDF, or a text editor. Your files
Notebook
Python a cell at a time, with gpxiltools, gpxil2pxil and pxil ready to import, and NumPy for the planes. The Notebook tab
Terminal
A bash terminal with the commands gpxil, gpxil2pxil and pxil, and the usual file and text commands. The terminal

The help

  • Search the help with the box at the top of this pane. Titles and keywords count most, so a short word such as planes, haplotype or pxil works well.
  • The book button next to the search box goes back to the list of all topics, and ← Back to the page you were reading.
  • Help for the … tab, under the search box, opens the help for the tab you’re in.
  • Drag the bar between the help and the tabs to make the help wider or narrower, or double-click it to put it back. The button at the top left, or Ctrl+\, hides and shows the help.
  • The ▶ buttons in the help do what they say: they open a file, a tool or an example with the right settings, so you can follow a walkthrough by pressing them in turn.
  • Boxes of code have their own buttons: Copy, Run in the terminal for commands, and Add to notebook for Python.

The buttons at the top

Saved in this browser
Your files are saved as you work. This says so, or warns you when the browser can’t keep them (in a private window, say) or they take up a lot of room. Where your files are kept
user
Put in your name, and the terminal shows it in place of user. It’s kept in this browser only. Your home folder is /home/user whatever the name.
My files
Download all your files as a .zip, add files or a folder from your computer, see where your files are kept, or start afresh by deleting them all.
Full screen
Uses the whole screen (F11 does too).

On a phone or a small screen

Everything works on a narrow screen, but there’s less room. The help slides in over the tabs when you open it with the button at the top left, and slides away when you tap outside it. On a very narrow screen, the tabs other than the one you’re in show only their icons. The Viewer’s controls stack above the picture. Large jobs, such as encoding a whole chromosome, are better done on a computer, which has more memory than a phone.
