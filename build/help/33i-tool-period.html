Wrap a repeat at its period

gpxil period wraps a tandem repeat – a microsatellite, a GA-sequence – at its period, one unit a row, so its units line up in columns and a unit that differs stands out. The reference is drawn beside the person’s two haplotypes, so you can see which units they have changed, gained or lost.

How it is wrapped

the period
For each step up to 50 bases, the share of the stretch’s bases that equal the base a step further on. The period is the shortest step whose share is within 0.05 of the best, so a repeat of AAAG is period 4, not 8. A stretch with no clear period – the best share under 0.6 – still gets the best, with a note. Or you give the period.
aligned
Each haplotype is drawn at the reference’s positions, so its rows are the reference’s rows: a substituted base is outlined, a deleted base is a dash, a base not known is in the colour of N, and an insertion is a bar on the right edge of the base it follows. A haplotype that isn’t there, as the second where a man has one X, is blank.
as sequences
Each haplotype is its own sequence, insertions in and deletions out, wrapped at the same period, so a unit gained or lost shifts the units after it, and the haplotype ends lower or higher than the reference.
as text
The reference and the haplotypes side by side, a unit a line; a substituted base is in lower case, a deleted one a dash, and an insertion a + after the unit, with its bases listed below.

The form

The gpxil file
A person’s file, or a reference alone. A file without its reference plane needs the reference too.
Region
CONTIG:START-END, counting from 1, of up to 20,000 bases. For a chromosome 17 file, the GA-sequence of the first button, to begin with.
Period
Empty: found.
The haplotypes
Aligned, or as their own sequences.
As
A picture, 8 pixels a base in any palette, or text.

A GA-sequence that began as an Alu’s tail

At chr17:6,793,028-6,793,302, 275 bases of A and G begin in the tail of an AluJb element: RepeatMasker calls most of them a simple repeat, (AGAA)n. Their period is 4: 85 % of the bases equal the base a unit on. Genome in a Bottle’s v4.2.1 benchmark doesn’t call them; the newer v5.0q, made from a whole assembly of HG002’s genome, does, and he is heterozygous for 15 substitutions, 2 insertions and 2 deletions there. Wrapped, haplotype 1 is the reference but for an insertion of three units, AGAA AGAA AGAA. Haplotype 2 has all the substitutions, 15 bases in 13 units, and each of those units becomes one the repeat already has – AAAG becomes AGAG, AGAA becomes AGAG – as a unit copied over its neighbour would; it also has an insertion of five units and two deletions of two bases. As sequences, haplotype 1 is 12 bases longer than the reference, and haplotype 2 16. Pure GA-sequences counts how often that happens.

In the terminal

gpxil period /data/examples/HG002_chr17_v5.0q.noref.gpxil.tif chr17:6,793,028-6,793,302 --reference /data/examples/HG002_chr17.gpxil.tif \
    -o ~/results/GA-period.png
gpxil period /data/examples/HG002_chr17_v5.0q.noref.gpxil.tif chr17:6,793,028-6,793,302 --reference /data/examples/HG002_chr17.gpxil.tif \
    --layout sequence | head -12

--period gives the period, --max-period the longest to look for, and --scale and --palette the picture’s squares and colours. Without -o, the text goes to the terminal.

In Python

from gpxiltools import GpxilFile
from gpxiltools.period import best_period, panels, text

with GpxilFile("/data/examples/HG002_chr17_v5.0q.noref.gpxil.tif", reference="/data/examples/HG002_chr17.gpxil.tif") as g:
    c, s, e = g.view(*g.locate(*g.region_of("chr17:6793028-6793302"), join=True))
    period, share, shares = best_period(c.read("ref", s, e))
    ps = panels(c, s, e)                          # (the reference, then each haplotype)
print(period, round(share, 2), {k: round(v, 2) for k, v in shares.items() if k <= 8})
print(text(ps, period)[:300])

picture(ps, period, scale=8, title=…) draws the picture, a Pillow image.
