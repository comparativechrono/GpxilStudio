The palettes

A gpxil file holds codes, not colours. A palette turns them into colours when a picture is drawn: into screen colours (sRGB) for a PNG, or directly into ink (CMYK percentages) for print, so neither is converted from the other. gpxil palettes lists them.

PaletteACGT
classicredbluegreenyellowthe colours of the original gpxil notebooks, and the default
okabe-itobluish greenblueorangevermilioncolours that people with any common colour-blindness can tell apart
gpxiblackdark greywhitelight greythe grey levels of Albrecht-Buehler’s genome pixel images, after which gpxil is named
processcyanmagentayellowblackone pure printing ink for each base, which prints most faithfully

Every palette also has a colour for N (an unknown base) and for “no base” (paper). The other codes are worked out from the four bases: an ambiguous base, or a heterozygous one in the genotype view, is the mean of its bases’ colours (in the process palette, the inks overprint), and a base in a soft-masked repeat is faded 45 % towards white (not in gpxi, whose greys need all their levels).

With the process palette, the code of a base is its ink: the four bits of a ref code, A, C, G and T, lay down cyan, magenta, yellow and black. A heterozygous A/G base prints cyan and yellow, green.
