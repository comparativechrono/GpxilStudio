The formats

The studio works with a handful of kinds of file. It tells them apart by their names, so keep the usual endings. gpxil is in the middle: a reference and a person’s variants go in, and pictures, books, gene and protein pictures, and FASTA and VCF again come out.

Besides these, the studio has notebooks (.ipynb), workflows (.workflow.json) and the records of their runs (workflow-run.json), and opens other text files, such as scripts, in the Files tab’s editor.

Why gpxil is a picture

A genome is a sequence of letters, and a sequence of letters can be laid out in rows, like text, with one pixel for each letter. Guenter Albrecht-Buehler drew genomes this way, in shades of grey, and found that repeats line up into patterns you can see. gpxil keeps the one-pixel-per-base picture but stores codes rather than colours, so that it holds the sequence exactly and a person’s genotype beside it, in ordinary TIFF files that any image software can open. pxil does the same for a single sequence, in a PNG.
