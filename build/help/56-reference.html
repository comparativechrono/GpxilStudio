The GRCh38 reference

/data/grch38 holds the human reference genome, GRCh38, as gpxil files: one for each chromosome, and one for the unplaced scaffolds. They are the Gpxil browser’s files, read from its web site a piece at a time as they are used, so they are there at once without being downloaded. Every tool and the Viewer can use them: as the reference to encode a VCF against, as a genome to look at or draw, or to make a gene’s pictures from.

What it is

  • The primary assembly of GRCh38, from Ensembl release 116 (Homo_sapiens.GRCh38.dna_sm.primary_assembly.fa.gz): chromosomes 1 to 22, X, Y and the mitochondrion, and 169 unplaced and unlocalised scaffolds, such as KI270728.1. There are no alternative haplotypes or patches.
  • Soft-masked: bases in repeats are lower case in Ensembl’s FASTA file, and marked as soft-masked in the ref plane, so the Viewer shows them paler.
  • The reference plane only, with no sample, written by gpxiltools 0.2.0 at 8,192 bases a row.
  • Named chr1 … chr22, chrX, chrY and chrM, with Ensembl’s names (1 … MT) as aliases, so a VCF that says 17 finds chr17. Each has its MD5 and refget checksums, which a file written without its reference plane is checked against.

Read from the web, a piece at a time

The files live on the Gpxil browser’s web site, comparativechrono.github.io/Gpxil-browser/data/grch38/. Nothing is downloaded whole: the Viewer and the Files tab fetch the strips they show, and Python reads a megabyte at a time, with HTTP range requests, as it needs to (Big files and memory). The first look at a chromosome takes a moment, and reading the reference needs a connection to the web.

A file of your own on GRCh38 written without its reference plane (--no-ref) borrows its bases from here: the Viewer does so by itself, the tools with Its reference, which they fill in for you, and gpxiltools with reference=. The checksums make sure it is the same reference.

Using it

  • To encode a VCF called on GRCh38: give the chromosome’s file to Make a gpxil file as the reference, a chromosome at a time (the walkthrough). Only the contigs encoded are read from it.
  • To look at: choose a chromosome in the Viewer, under GRCh38 reference.
  • To draw or print: gpxil render and gpxil book take any region of it.
  • For genes: gpxil2pxil makes any gene’s, transcript’s or protein’s picture from it, such as CFTR’s from chr7.gpxil.tif.
gpxil info /data/grch38/chr21.gpxil.tif
gpxil fasta /data/grch38/chr17.gpxil.tif --region chr17:7676101-7676200
gpxil render /data/grch38/chrM.gpxil.tif chrM -o ~/results/chrM.png --width 128 --scale 2

Its genes

The Gpxil browser also has the genes of Ensembl release 116 on GRCh38 – all 78,941 of them, and their 646,577 transcripts – with the canonical and MANE transcripts marked, and HGNC’s symbols, names and aliases. gpxil2pxil reads them a gene at a time when you name a gene (Genes, transcripts and proteins as pxil pictures). To work without the web, or with another annotation, give it a GTF file instead.

The whole genome in one file

For your own computer, the Gpxil browser offers the whole of GRCh38 as one gpxil file, GRCh38.gpxil.tif (848.5 MB), with its SHA-256 checksum beside it. Download it from the command line, check it, and use it like any other gpxil file:

curl -LO https://github.com/comparativechrono/Gpxil-browser/releases/download/grch38-ensembl116/GRCh38.gpxil.tif
curl -LO https://github.com/comparativechrono/Gpxil-browser/releases/download/grch38-ensembl116/GRCh38.gpxil.tif.sha256
sha256sum -c GRCh38.gpxil.tif.sha256          # on a Mac: shasum -a 256 -c GRCh38.gpxil.tif.sha256
gpxil info GRCh38.gpxil.tif

If the download stops, the same command with -C - after curl carries on from where it stopped. A chromosome at a time comes from the files the studio reads, such as curl -O https://comparativechrono.github.io/Gpxil-browser/data/grch38/gpxil/chr21.gpxil.tif. With the whole genome on your own computer, gpxil encode GRCh38.gpxil.tif -v my.vcf.gz --callable my.bed --no-ref -o my.gpxil.tif encodes a person’s whole genome in one go (On your own computer). In the studio there is no need: it reads the reference from the web as it is.

Where it comes from

GRCh38 is the Genome Reference Consortium’s assembly of the human genome. The sequence, its soft-masking and the genes are Ensembl’s, release 116, and the gene names HGNC’s. The Gpxil browser made the gpxil files with gpxiltools 0.2.0. Cite the assembly and Ensembl when you use them:
